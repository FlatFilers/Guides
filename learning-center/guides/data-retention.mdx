## Introduction

Data retention policies are crucial for managing and controlling the lifecycle
of data within your Flatfile account. In this guide, we'll walk you through the
process of adding and configuring data retention policies based on our two
policy types: `last activity` and `since created`. These policies will
help you automatically clean up outdated data within your Flatfile environment.

Flatfile will expire Spaces (the Space is no longer accessible, though it
remains listed among your Spaces). Expiring a Space drops the underlying storage
of its non-frozen Workbooks in that Space — frozen Workbooks are retained — and
this cannot be recovered. The safeguards described below exist to make this
outcome predictable.

When you create a policy or tighten an existing one, its retention clock starts
at the moment of the change. Tightening means creating a new policy, changing an
existing policy's type, or shortening its period. Rather than expiring your
existing Spaces on the next scheduled run, the policy waits one full retention
period from the change before the first expiry, so you have time to confirm the
policy is correct before any Spaces are expired.
Policies that predate this change, and that you haven't modified, keep their
prior timing.

<Note>
  By default, data retention is set to 365 days. The execution window for data
  deletion may vary by +/- 7 days from your defined period. A new policy's
  retention period must be a whole number of at least 7 days. An existing policy
  already below 7 days can still be updated, as long as the change does not
  shorten it further.
</Note>

## Understanding Data Deletion Logic

Here's how the timing works for each policy type:

### Since Created Policy Example

- Policy created on January 1st.
- Policy set to 7 days.
- Existing Spaces are not expired on the next scheduled run. Instead, one full
  retention period after the policy is created — on January 8th — any Space
  older than 7 days is expired and its data is deleted. From then on, Spaces
  expire on the regular schedule once they pass 7 days since creation.

### Last Activity Policy Example

- Policy created on January 1st.
- Policy set to 14 days.
- Existing Spaces are not expired on the next scheduled run. Instead, one full
  retention period after the policy is created — on January 15th — any Space
  that hasn't been modified in more than 14 days is expired and its data is
  deleted. Ongoing expiry then follows the 14-day period on the regular
  schedule.

## Monitoring and Adjusting Policies

Regularly monitor the results of the data retention policies to ensure they
align with your data management goals. If needed, adjust the policy time periods
based on your evolving data requirements.

## Implementing via API

If you'd like to create, update, or delete your policy via API, check out our
[API reference](https://reference.flatfile.com/).

Some changes require you to confirm how many Spaces they will expire before
Flatfile accepts them:

- This applies when a change creates a policy, changes its type, or shortens it,
  and its first run would expire 1,000 or more Spaces.
- Set the `acknowledgedSpaceCount` field to the number of Spaces the change
  would expire.
- The value must match the number Flatfile calculates within a 5% tolerance, or
  the request is rejected.
- You don't need to calculate this in advance: submit the change, and if
  confirmation is required, the rejection reports the exact value to send back in
  `acknowledgedSpaceCount`.
- Loosening a policy, or leaving it unchanged, never requires this field.
